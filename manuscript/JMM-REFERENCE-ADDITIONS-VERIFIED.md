# Additional verified reference candidates for the JMM manuscript

This is a screened working list, not a final bibliography. Each reference was
checked through its PubMed record and has a precise potential use. Include an
item in the final manuscript only when it supports a specific author-written
sentence.

1. S. Mukherjee, T. E. Balius, and R. C. Rizzo, “Docking validation
   resources: protein family and ligand flexibility experiments,” *Journal of
   Chemical Information and Modeling*, vol. 50, no. 11, pp. 1986–2000, 2010,
   doi: 10.1021/ci1001982.
   - Potential use: contextual support for retaining a defined validation set
     and reporting pose-recovery evaluation conditions.
   - Verification record: PubMed PMID 21033739.

2. D. Ramírez and J. Caballero, “Is It Reliable to Take the Molecular Docking
   Top Scoring Position as the Best Solution without Considering Available
   Structural Data?” *Molecules*, vol. 23, no. 5, Art. no. 1038, 2018,
   doi: 10.3390/molecules23051038.
   - Potential use: contextual support for reporting both top-score and
     geometry-based pose-recovery values rather than conflating them.
   - Verification record: PubMed PMID 29710787.

3. W. J. Allen and R. C. Rizzo, “Implementation of the Hungarian algorithm to
   account for ligand symmetry and similarity in structure-based design,”
   *Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling*, vol. 54, no. 2, pp.
   518–529, 2014, doi: 10.1021/ci400534h.
   - Potential use: explain why atom correspondence and molecular symmetry
     must be declared when reporting RMSD.
   - Verification record: PubMed PMID 24410429.

4. F. Stanzione, I. Giangreco, and J. C. Cole, “Use of molecular docking
   computational tools in drug discovery,” *Progress in Medicinal Chemistry*,
   vol. 60, pp. 273–343, 2021, doi: 10.1016/bs.pmch.2021.01.004.
   - Potential use: general docking-workflow context only.
   - Verification record: PubMed PMID 34147204.

## Scope limit

None of these references substantiates affinity, activity, selectivity, or
therapeutic effects for the BRAF or KIF11 examples. They support methodology
and reporting choices only.
